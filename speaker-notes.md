# Speaker notes

The complete bilingual talk track lives in [`Keeping a Platform Alive Speaker Guide.docx`](Keeping%20a%20Platform%20Alive%20Speaker%20Guide.docx). These notes preserve the narrative anchors.

## Opening

“A platform stays alive when it can listen after release, make an accountable decision, and return a verified outcome to the person who raised the signal.”

The original idea was an in-product floating button. The mechanism changed, but not the principle: capture feedback close to where people experience the problem.

## Intake and context

Slack already contains the people, clarifications, urgency, and evidence around a report. A report can start small: observation, context, impact, and evidence are enough to begin.

Automation reads the report and its thread, checks for duplicates, then creates or enriches the Linear issue. RedLens and Pedaxis use distinct harnesses and teams, but share this operating principle.

## Decision and triage

The thread is the evolving context; Linear is the decision record. An assignee, scope, state, and evidence make the work actionable.

Triage is not a classification exercise. It collects the signal, assesses impact, and chooses an executable next step.

## Delivery and validation

Estimate cost, effort, and risk before delivery is committed. Reproduce the report, isolate the cause, change it, verify it, review it, and only then ship.

## Closure and accountability

The closure mock makes the end-to-end return visible: the Developer posts the estimate and a short implementation update, the Reviewer posts PASS, Linear records the resolved issue, and the result returns to the original Slack thread.

Automation removes repetitive coordination; people still prioritise, approve scope, and accept the evidence. The final pattern is a loop: listen, decide, deliver, and return with evidence.
